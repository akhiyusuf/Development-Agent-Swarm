# App idea: African Nutrition & Progressive Calisthenics/Pilates Tracker

## Core concept
A mobile app combining (1) calorie, macro, and micronutrient tracking and (2) a
workout app for calisthenics and Pilates, built specifically for African users —
a group chronically underserved by existing nutrition and fitness apps.

## Differentiator 1: African ingredient database
Mainstream calorie trackers (MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, etc.) have thin or
nonexistent coverage of African staple ingredients and dishes — many are
missing entirely or only present as inaccurate generic placeholders. This app
needs first-class nutrition data covering popular African ingredients and
prepared dishes, including:
- Full macro breakdown (calories, protein, carbs, fat) per ingredient/dish
- Micronutrient data (vitamins, minerals) where available
- Regional dish variants across African cuisines (which regions to prioritize
  for v1 is an open question below — West, East, Southern, North African
  cuisines all have distinct staples)
- Logging by individual ingredient or by common prepared meal

## Differentiator 2: Progression-gated workout system
The workout side isn't a flat exercise list — moves are gated behind
prerequisite skill/strength achievements, like a skill tree. A user can't
unlock an advanced move until they've demonstrated the underlying baseline.

Illustrative example only (not necessarily the correct real progression —
this needs proper validation): holding a freestanding handstand for a minimum
duration before unlocking handstand push-ups.

- Covers calisthenics and Pilates specifically
- Progressions must be real and evidence-based, not invented — this is a core
  research task, not something to assume
- App tracks each user's current unlocked tier and the time/rep thresholds
  they've hit

## Open questions for the research stage
- Which African regions/cuisines to prioritize for v1 ingredient coverage —
  covering all of Africa exhaustively in one release isn't realistic. Nigeria
  must be included as one of the first countries covered in v1; which other
  countries/regions join it is open for research to determine.
- Where to source nutrition data for African ingredients, since many aren't
  in standard databases (USDA, etc.) — likely needs regional food composition
  tables or academic sources
- The actual correct, safe progression trees for calisthenics and Pilates,
  validated against real strength-training and Pilates progression standards
- Whether nutrition and workout should be one unified app or two connected
  modules within it

## Constraints
- Target users: African users (including diaspora) currently underserved by
  mainstream fitness/nutrition apps
- Mobile-first
